\section{Forecast-optimal smoothness}

\subsection{Continuation of the fitted trend}

A trend estimate does not become a forecast until a continuation rule is specified. In the baseline model, the fitted finite-difference structure is continued by imposing zero future $d$th differences. This yields a fixed linear operator
\[
G_{d,h}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times L}
\]
such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forecast_operator}
\widehat z_T(\lambda)
=
G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T.
\end{equation}

Equivalently, using backward differences at the end of the fitted trend,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:continuation}
\widehat\tau_{T+k\mid T}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}
\binom{k+j-1}{j}
\nabla^j\widehat\tau_T,
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,h.
\end{equation}
For $d=1$ the continuation is constant; for $d=2$ it is linear; and for $d=3$ it is quadratic. This continuation is the extrapolation implied by the same finite-difference structure that defines the fitted trend.

\subsection{Rolling future-block loss}

Let
\[
z_T=(y_{T+1},\ldots,y_{T+h})^\top
\]
be the realized future block at origin $T$. For candidate $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:origin_loss}
f_T(\lambda)
=
\frac{1}{h}
\left\|
z_T-G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T
\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}

For chronological origins $\mathcal O=\{T_1,\ldots,T_M\}$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:f_lambda}
f_{d,L,h}(\lambda)
=
\frac{1}{M}
\sum_{m=1}^{M}f_{T_m}(\lambda).
\end{equation}
No observation after $T_m$ may affect the fit or forecast at that origin; future observations are revealed only for scoring.

Because $S(\lambda)$ is one-to-one, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F_S}
F_{d,L,h}(S)
=
f_{d,L,h}(\lambda(S)),
\qquad
S\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
The proposed selector is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Sstar}
\boxed{
S^\star_{d,L,h}
\in
\arg\min_{S\in[0,1]}
F_{d,L,h}(S).
}
\end{equation}

We call \eqref{eq:Sstar} \emph{forecast-optimal smoothness}. Optimality is conditional on the specified PLS family, difference order, window length, continuation rule, horizon, rolling-origin design, and loss.

\subsection{Why future blocks rather than only one-step errors}

A one-step criterion is appropriate when the operational task is one-step forecasting. For longer horizons, however, the smoothness choice affects both terminal derivatives and how those derivatives accumulate under extrapolation. The relevant tuning target can therefore change with $h$.

The future-block construction makes this dependence explicit. If the decision problem evaluates $h$ future observations jointly, smoothness is selected against that same block rather than against a reconstruction surrogate.

\subsection{Relationship to conventional cross-validation}

The criterion in \eqref{eq:F_S} evaluates observations excluded from the fit at each origin, but it is neither ordinary leave-one-out CV nor GCV. Classical smoothing CV/GCV usually evaluates reconstruction under the same sample geometry; the proposed criterion evaluates extrapolation into a genuinely later block.

This also separates the proposal from Hart's TSCV \cite{hart1994tscv}. Both use predictive performance to tune smoothness, but Hart's object is a kernel bandwidth under one-step prediction with a time-series error model. Here the object is normalized finite-difference smoothness and the forecast is the native continuation of the penalized trend over an arbitrary horizon.
