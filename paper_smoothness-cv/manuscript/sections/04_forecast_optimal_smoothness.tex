\section{Forecast-optimal smoothness by chronological cross-validation}
\label{sec:forecast_cv}

\subsection{Continuation of the fitted trend}

A trend estimate does not become a forecast until a continuation rule is specified. In the baseline model, the fitted finite-difference structure is continued by imposing zero future $d$th differences. This yields a fixed linear operator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:continuation_operator_space}
G_{d,h}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times L}
\end{equation}
such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forecast_operator}
\widehat z_T(\lambda)
=
G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T.
\end{equation}

Equivalently, using backward differences at the end of the fitted trend,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:continuation}
\widehat\tau_{T+k\mid T}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}
\binom{k+j-1}{j}
\nabla^j\widehat\tau_T,
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,h.
\end{equation}
For $d=1$ the continuation is constant; for $d=2$ it is linear; and for $d=3$ it is quadratic.

\subsection{Origin-specific forecast-loss surfaces}

Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:future_observation_block}
z_T=(y_{T+1},\ldots,y_{T+h})^\top
\end{equation}
be the realized future block at origin $T$. For candidate $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:origin_loss}
f_T(\lambda)
=
\frac{1}{h}
\left\|
z_T-G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T
\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Expanding the loss clarifies how $H_\lambda$ enters the forecast-MSE surface:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:origin_loss_quadratic}
h f_T(\lambda)
= z_T^\top z_T
- 2z_T^\top G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T
+ x_T^\top H_\lambda G_{d,h}^\top G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T.
\end{equation}
In particular, this is a predictive loss, not the in-sample residual
norm $\|x_T-H_\lambda x_T\|_2^2$. Because $S(\lambda)$ is
one-to-one, we write the same surface in normalized smoothness as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:origin_loss_smoothness}
F_T(S)=f_T(\lambda(S)),
\qquad
S\in[0,1].
\end{equation}

At a chronological sequence of historical forecast origins $T_1,\ldots,T_M$, each surface can have one or several local minima. Denote the local-minimum set at origin $T_m$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local_minimum_set}
\mathcal M_m
=
\{S_{1,m},\ldots,S_{K_m,m}\}.
\end{equation}

This sequence of local-minimum sets is the central dynamic object of the paper.

\subsection{Horizon-matched pooled forecast cross-validation}

The proposed pooled criterion averages forecast losses across historical origins for fixed $(d,L,h)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F_S}
F^{\mathrm{pool}}_{d,L,h}(S)
=
\frac{1}{M}
\sum_{m=1}^{M}F_{T_m}(S).
\end{equation}
Its minimizer is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Sstar}
\widehat S^{\mathrm{pool}}_{d,L,h}
\in
\arg\min_{S\in[0,1]}
F^{\mathrm{pool}}_{d,L,h}(S).
\end{equation}

This is the primary forecast-optimal smoothness selector. It chooses the same smoothness across the current historical validation origins but can choose a different value for each horizon $h$. It requires neither minimum tracking nor the optional branch state.

\subsection{Mandatory final refit and untouched evaluation}

Neither the pooled selector nor any optional adaptive rule retains an old fitted trend. Once the current smoothness $\widehat S_T$ has been selected, all intermediate validation fits are discarded and the trend is refit using the most recent information available at the outer origin:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:final_refit}
\widehat\tau_T
=
H_{\lambda(\widehat S_T)}
y_{T-L+1:T}.
\end{equation}
The operational forecast is then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:final_operational_forecast}
\widehat y_{T+1:T+h\mid T}
=
G_{d,h}\widehat\tau_T.
\end{equation}

The pooled method averages historical validation losses, not historical trend estimates. Optional adaptive rules may also average historical smoothness values, but must use the same final-refit procedure.

\subsection{Relationship to conventional cross-validation}

Unlike ordinary leave-one-out CV and GCV, the proposed loss directly measures chronological extrapolation over the declared horizon. \Cref{sec:optional_dynamic} separately explores time-adaptive use of individual local minima.

Hart's TSCV \cite{hart1994tscv} remains an important predictive-smoothing precedent. The proposed application differs in its finite-difference PLS estimator, normalized smoothness parameterization, multi-step continuation, and forecast-MSE loss surface. Temporal branch tracking is optional, not required for forecast-CV.
