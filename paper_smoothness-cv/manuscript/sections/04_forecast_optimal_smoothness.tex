\section{Forecast-optimal smoothness}

\subsection{Continuation of the fitted trend}

A trend estimate does not become a forecast until a continuation rule is specified. In the baseline model, the fitted finite-difference structure is continued by imposing zero future $d$th differences. This yields a fixed linear operator
\[
G_{d,h}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times L}
\]
such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forecast_operator}
\widehat z_T(\lambda)
=
G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T.
\end{equation}

Equivalently, using backward differences at the end of the fitted trend,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:continuation}
\widehat\tau_{T+k\mid T}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}
\binom{k+j-1}{j}
\nabla^j\widehat\tau_T,
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,h.
\end{equation}
For $d=1$ the continuation is constant; for $d=2$ it is linear; and for $d=3$ it is quadratic.

\subsection{Origin-specific forecast-loss surfaces}

Let
\[
z_T=(y_{T+1},\ldots,y_{T+h})^\top
\]
be the realized future block at origin $T$. For candidate $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:origin_loss}
f_T(\lambda)
=
\frac{1}{h}
\left\|
z_T-G_{d,h}H_\lambda x_T
\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Because $S(\lambda)$ is one-to-one, we write the same surface in normalized smoothness as
\[
F_T(S)=f_T(\lambda(S)),
\qquad
S\in[0,1].
\]

At a chronological sequence of historical forecast origins $T_1,\ldots,T_M$, each surface can have one or several local minima. Denote the local-minimum set at origin $T_m$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local_minimum_set}
\mathcal M_m
=
\{S_{1,m},\ldots,S_{K_m,m}\}.
\end{equation}

This sequence of local-minimum sets is the central dynamic object of the paper.

\subsection{Pooled forecast-CV as a baseline}

A natural static baseline averages forecast losses across historical origins:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F_S}
F^{\mathrm{pool}}_{d,L,h}(S)
=
\frac{1}{M}
\sum_{m=1}^{M}F_{T_m}(S).
\end{equation}
Its minimizer is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Sstar}
\widehat S^{\mathrm{pool}}_{d,L,h}
\in
\arg\min_{S\in[0,1]}
F^{\mathrm{pool}}_{d,L,h}(S).
\end{equation}

This selector asks which single smoothness value has the lowest average historical forecast loss. It remains an important benchmark, but it discards the temporal organization of the local minima themselves.

\subsection{Tracking local minima through time}

To retain that organization, we connect nearby local minima across adjacent forecast origins. A current minimum $S_{j,m}$ may continue a previous branch when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:epsilon_tracking}
|S_{j,m}-S_{j,m-1}|
\le
\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
subject to one-to-one matching at each origin.

The radius $\varepsilon$ does not define a fixed exogenous region such as ``smoothness near 0.9.'' Instead, branch locations are determined by the data through the recovered local minima; $\varepsilon$ only controls whether minima on neighboring surfaces are close enough to be treated as the same temporal branch.

For a tracked branch $j$, let $\ell^{(1)}_{j,m}$ denote its Validation-1 forecast loss at the origin where the local minimum is identified. The trend is then refit through Validation 1 using the same $(d,L,S_{j,m})$, and the immediately following block is scored to obtain $\ell^{(2)}_{j,m}$, the Validation-2 forecast loss.

We collect the branch history as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:branch_matrix}
V_j
=
\begin{pmatrix}
S_{j,t_1} & \ell^{(1)}_{j,t_1} & \ell^{(2)}_{j,t_1}\\
S_{j,t_2} & \ell^{(1)}_{j,t_2} & \ell^{(2)}_{j,t_2}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Branch selection and current smoothness}

The forecasting decision is separated into two steps. First, a historical branch-selection rule
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:branch_selector}
\widehat j_T
=
\psi(V_1,\ldots,V_J)
\end{equation}
chooses among persistent branches using information available before the outer test. A simple baseline prefers branches with maximal support and then minimizes mean historical Validation-2 loss.

Second, a branch-to-smoothness rule
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phi_rule}
\widehat S_T
=
\phi(V_{\widehat j_T})
\end{equation}
converts the selected branch history into the smoothness used at the current forecast origin.

The simplest rule is the newest tracked minimum,
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{last}}(V_j)=S_{j,T}.
\]
Alternative stabilizing rules use the recent mean or median,
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{mean},K}(V_j)
=
\frac1K
\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}
S_{j,T-r},
\]
or
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{med},K}(V_j)
=
\operatorname{median}
\{S_{j,T-K+1},\ldots,S_{j,T}\}.
\]

An alternative weighted mean uses recency alone. Let $r=0$ denote the newest tracked smoothness value, with older values indexed by increasing $r$. Then
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{recency}}(V_j)
=
\frac{
\sum_{r=0}^{R}\rho^r S_{j,T-r}
}{
\sum_{r=0}^{R}\rho^r
},
\qquad
0<\rho<1.
\]
The newest value receives weight one and older values are exponentially discounted. For interpretability we parameterize $\rho$ by a half-life $H$,
\[
\rho=2^{-1/H},
\]
so the weight assigned to a value $H$ tracked origins old is one half of the newest weight.

A loss-weighted rule gives more influence to historically predictive values:
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{V2}}(V_j)
=
\frac{
\sum_t w_{j,t}S_{j,t}
}{
\sum_t w_{j,t}
},
\qquad
w_{j,t}
=
\frac{1}{\ell^{(2)}_{j,t}+\delta},
\]
with $\delta>0$ used only for numerical stabilization. Recency may be incorporated through
\[
w_{j,t}
=
\frac{\rho^{T-t}}{\ell^{(2)}_{j,t}+\delta},
\qquad
0<\rho\le1.
\]

A distinct class of maps treats the branch smoothness values as a series to be extrapolated. For a branch with values $S_{j,t_1},\ldots,S_{j,t_m}$, fit the auxiliary trend
\[
(\widehat a,\widehat b)
\in
\arg\min_{a,b}
\sum_{u=1}^{m}
\omega_u\bigl(S_{j,t_u}-a-bu\bigr)^2.
\]
The resulting branch-based smoothness decision is
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{trend}}(V_j)
=
\Pi_{[0,1]}\bigl(\widehat a+\widehat b(m+1)\bigr),
\]
where $\Pi_{[0,1]}$ truncates to the admissible normalized-smoothness interval. Uniform weights, recent-window weights, or exponentially increasing recency weights lead to different examples. Alternatively, one can extrapolate the branch increments:
\[
\phi_{\Delta,\rho}(V_j)
=
\Pi_{[0,1]}
\left[
S_{j,t_m}
+
\frac{
\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}
\rho^r\bigl(S_{j,t_{m-r}}-S_{j,t_{m-r-1}}\bigr)
}{
\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}\rho^r
}
\right].
\]
These auxiliary models forecast the next \emph{smoothness value}, not the underlying observations or an old fitted trend. The final observation forecast still follows the fresh-refit procedure below.

The examples above are not intended to define a tournament for one universally best smoothness rule. Their role is to show that the tracked branch state $V_j$ supports a family of admissible decision maps
\[
\phi:V_j\mapsto \widehat S_T.
\]
Different applications may emphasize persistence, recency, historical forecast performance, robustness, or forward extrapolation of the smoothness trajectory. Empirical comparisons describe the behavior of these choices under controlled conditions; they do not make one functional part of the definition of the framework.

\subsection{Mandatory final refit}

Neither the pooled selector nor the dynamic branch rules retain an old fitted trend. Once the current smoothness $\widehat S_T$ has been selected, all intermediate validation fits are discarded and the trend is refit using the most recent information available at the outer origin:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:final_refit}
\widehat\tau_T
=
H_{\lambda(\widehat S_T)}
y_{T-L+1:T}.
\end{equation}
The operational forecast is then
\[
\widehat y_{T+1:T+h\mid T}
=
G_{d,h}\widehat\tau_T.
\]

Thus the proposed dynamic method can average historical losses or smoothness values when a declared decision rule requires it, but it never averages historical fitted trends.

\subsection{Relationship to conventional cross-validation}

The construction remains distinct from ordinary leave-one-out CV and GCV. The relevant losses are chronological extrapolation losses, and the dynamic extension additionally studies persistence of local minima across successive forecast-loss surfaces.

Hart's TSCV \cite{hart1994tscv} remains an important predictive-smoothing precedent. The present problem differs in estimator, normalized smoothness coordinate, multi-step continuation, multimodal objective geometry, and the explicit temporal tracking of local smoothness minima.
