\section{Evaluation protocol}

The empirical evaluation is designed to test whether forecast-oriented smoothness selection changes the selected trend and improves subsequent forecasts. The simulation design in this section was frozen after the exploratory Checkpoints 01--02 and before the paper-scale Checkpoint 03 run.

\subsection{Simulation questions}

The controlled simulation study addresses three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does horizon-matched forecast-CV select a different amount of smoothness from one-step forecast tuning and conventional in-sample selectors?
\item When the selected smoothness differs, does the horizon-matched choice improve genuinely later forecast loss?
\item How far are the feasible selectors from two simulation-only targets: historical trend-recovery optimality and latent future forecast optimality?
\end{enumerate}

The primary design fixes the difference order at
\[
d=2
\]
and the estimation-window length at
\[
L=60.
\]
The operational forecast horizons are
\[
h\in\{1,3,6,12\}.
\]
Keeping $d$ and $L$ fixed is deliberate: the paper studies smoothness selection conditional on a forecasting configuration rather than joint adaptation of $(d,L,S)$.

\subsection{Latent mechanisms and noise}

Five latent trend mechanisms are retained in the frozen paper-scale design:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a linear null-space control;
\item a smoothly nonlinear trend;
\item an oscillatory trend with repeated local slope changes;
\item a recent slope change;
\item a terminal bend with local end-of-sample curvature.
\end{enumerate}

The retained set spans a simple correctly aligned control and several forms of local shape misspecification for linear continuation. Quadratic and turning-point mechanisms used during the exploratory refinement stage were excluded before the paper-scale run because they were structurally redundant with the retained smooth nonlinear and terminal-curvature cases.

Observation noise is generated under three conditions: independent Gaussian noise, persistent AR(1) noise, and finite-variance Student-$t$ noise as a heavy-tail robustness condition. The marginal noise standard deviation is set to $0.3$ or $0.7$.

The paper-scale simulation uses $100$ random seeds and series length $N=360$. For each generated series, the common outer forecast origins are
\[
T\in\{228,252,276,300,324,348\}.
\]
The same origins are used for every horizon. At each origin, all tuning uses only observations available at or before $T$; the future block is revealed only for scoring.

\subsection{Smoothness search}

The scientific criterion is evaluated on a deterministic grid of $601$ equally spaced smoothness values on $[0,1]$. A dense grid is sufficient here because numerical efficiency is not the contribution of this paper. The companion numerical-methods paper studies more efficient recovery of multimodal minima.

\subsection{Selection versus final refitting}

The rolling validation exercise selects the smoothness hyperparameter, not one of the intermediate fitted trends. At outer forecast origin \(T\), each candidate \(S\) is scored over historical pseudo-out-of-sample validation blocks contained entirely within \(y_{1:T}\). Let
\[
\widehat S_{T,h}
\in
\arg\min_S F_{T,h}(S)
\]
denote the selected smoothness. The fold-specific trend estimates used to construct \(F_{T,h}\) are then discarded.

Before forecasting the untouched outer block, the trend is re-estimated using the most recent full estimation window available at the outer origin:
\[
x_T=y_{T-L+1:T},
\qquad
\widehat\tau_T
=
H_{\lambda(\widehat S_{T,h})}x_T.
\]
The operational forecast is therefore
\[
\widehat y_{T+1:T+h\mid T}
=
G_{d,h}\widehat\tau_T.
\]
Thus the inner folds average forecast losses for candidate values of \(S\); they do not average trend estimates. When the evaluation advances to a later outer origin, observations that have become available are incorporated into the new historical information set and the full select-\(S\), refit, and forecast cycle is repeated.

\subsection{Competing selectors}

The primary feasible comparison set is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item horizon-matched future-block forecast-CV defined in \eqref{eq:F_S};
\item one-step rolling forecast-CV, whose selected smoothness is then used at the operational horizon;
\item ordinary linear-smoother CV;
\item GCV;
\item AICc.
\end{enumerate}

The CV/GCV/AICc criteria follow the PLS comparison studied by Cort\'es-Toto et al.\ \cite{cortes2017smoothness}. BIC was also audited during the exploratory stage, but under a global search over the full smoothness path its published score was pinned to the left finite boundary in every audited criterion curve. Because a defensible additional search-domain restriction would need to be introduced externally, BIC is not treated as a primary feasible comparator in the frozen paper-scale experiment.

The one-step versus horizon-matched comparison isolates the main forecasting question. For $h>1$, the same finite-difference trend family is tuned once using one-step forecast loss and once using the same $h$-step future block used operationally.

\subsection{Simulation-only oracle targets}

Two non-feasible selectors are retained for interpretation.

The historical recovery oracle is
\[
S_{\mathrm{rec}}
\in
\arg\min_S
\left\|
\widehat\tau_S-\tau
\right\|^2,
\]
where the latent historical trend is known only because the data are simulated.

The latent future forecast oracle is
\[
S_{\mathrm{oracle},h}
\in
\arg\min_S
\left\|
\tau_{T+1:T+h}
-
G_{2,h}H_{\lambda(S)}y_{T-L+1:T}
\right\|^2.
\]
The historical fit still uses noisy observations; only the oracle choice of $S$ is allowed to see the latent future. These two oracles separate trend-recovery optimality from forecast optimality and are not presented as practical forecasting competitors.

\subsection{Primary performance summaries}

For each seed, trend mechanism, noise model, noise scale, horizon, and selector, outer-origin losses are first averaged to obtain a series-level MSFE. Paired method comparisons then use
\[
\log
\frac{\operatorname{MSFE}_{\mathrm{forecast-CV},h}}
     {\operatorname{MSFE}_{\mathrm{comparator}}}.
\]
For scale-free aggregation we report the geometric RMSFE ratio
\[
\exp\left[
\frac12
E\left\{
\log
\frac{\operatorname{MSFE}_{\mathrm{forecast-CV},h}}
     {\operatorname{MSFE}_{\mathrm{comparator}}}
\right\}
\right].
\]
Values below one favor horizon-matched forecast-CV.

The simulation uses common random numbers across trend mechanisms within each seed/noise-model/noise-scale combination. Consequently, uncertainty intervals are obtained by nonparametric bootstrap resampling of seeds rather than individual scenario rows. Results are also reported separately by noise model when the iid, dependent, and heavy-tailed conditions differ materially.

Secondary summaries include MAE, selected smoothness $S$, effective degrees of freedom from \eqref{eq:edf}, endpoint-selection rates, and the gaps between feasible forecast-CV, the recovery oracle, and the latent future forecast oracle.

\subsection{Public real-data evaluation}

The submission-stage empirical analysis will complement the controlled simulations with a broad public multi-series forecasting panel. The real-data protocol must preserve the same chronological logic: tuning occurs only on historical information, forecast horizons are fixed in advance, and no series-specific manual tuning is allowed.

A public competition-style panel such as the M4 data is a suitable primary candidate because it provides many heterogeneous series and established forecast horizons. Any seasonal preprocessing must be prespecified so that the paper remains about trend smoothness rather than seasonal-model selection.

A small real-time macroeconomic illustration may be added separately. Historical macroeconomic backtests require historical vintages; revised current histories are not treated as if they were available at earlier forecast origins.

\subsection{Result-freeze rule}

The paper-scale simulation design---seeds, mechanisms, noise conditions, horizons, window length, forecast origins, smoothness grid, and primary feasible comparator set---is frozen before inspecting the Checkpoint 03 paper-preset results. These quantities are not altered afterward because of favorable or unfavorable performance.
