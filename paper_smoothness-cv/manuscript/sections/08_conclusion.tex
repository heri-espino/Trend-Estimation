\section{Conclusion}

The choice of smoothness for a penalized trend is a prediction
decision whenever the fitted trend will be extrapolated. We therefore
formulate a chronological, horizon-specific cross-validation
criterion for finite-difference penalized least squares. For each
candidate normalized smoothness value, the historical window is
smoothed using $H_{\lambda(S)}$, extrapolated through the declared
$h$-step continuation operator $G_{d,h}$, and scored on the subsequent
realized validation block. The method chooses the value minimizing
pooled historical forecast mean squared error:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:conclusion_core_cv}
\widehat S^{\mathrm{FCV}}_{T,d,L,h}
\in\arg\min_{S\in[0,1]}
\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}
\frac1h\left\|y_{t_m+1:t_m+h}
-G_{d,h}H_{\lambda(S)}x_{t_m}\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
After selection, the operational trend is refitted on the latest
available data before the untouched future block is forecast.
This definition does not require any adaptive tracking of
local minima.

The penalized smoother, trace-based index, and general idea
of predictive validation are not new individually. The contribution
examined here is their explicit assembly as a horizon-matched
trend-extrapolation selection procedure, together with its
performance assessment against classical and one-step smoothing
criteria. The controlled pooled-selector simulation supports the
value of matching the tuning horizon to the forecast objective,
particularly at longer horizons.

A second, numerical issue arises because the forecast-loss surface
can be multimodal. Finding the correct global candidate requires
recovery of potentially multiple interior minima and comparison
with the endpoints. This is investigated in the companion
numerical-methods study, where adaptive derivative bracketing
and Brent refinement are implemented; Sturm-based certification
remains a possible future extension.

Finally, a separate time-adaptive formulation tracks local-minimum
histories $V_j$ and allows branch-selection and smoothness mappings:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:conclusion_branch_decisions}
\widehat j_T=\psi(V_1,\ldots,V_J),
\qquad
\widehat S_T=\phi(V_{\widehat j_T}).
\end{equation}
This is an optional hyperparameter-selection mechanism for
settings in which past favorable smoothing regimes plausibly
inform future ones. Recent means, loss weights, and smoothness
extrapolations embody different assumptions; no universal rule
is implied. The small confirmation experiment favored one
recency rule, but larger financial and changing-roughness
evaluations did not establish its superiority over pooled CV.

These findings separate three issues: defining smoothness for
forecasting, solving the resulting multimodal optimization problem,
and optionally adapting the hyperparameter over time. The first
is the central statistical question of this paper; the second
belongs to its numerical companion; and the third remains an
application-dependent extension with clearly delimited
empirical support.
