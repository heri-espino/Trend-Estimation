\section{Conclusion}

Controlled-smoothness penalized trend estimation makes the strength of regularization interpretable, but forecasting introduces a further issue: the forecast-loss surface can be multimodal and its local optima can evolve through time. This paper therefore treats smoothness selection as a dynamic problem rather than only as the minimization of one pooled cross-validation curve.

The static pooled selector remains a benchmark:
\[
\widehat S^{\mathrm{pool}}_{T,h}
\in
\arg\min_S F^{\mathrm{pool}}_{T,h}(S).
\]
The dynamic formulation instead follows the local minima of successive forecast-loss surfaces, collects each persistent branch in a state matrix containing smoothness, Validation-1 loss, and subsequent Validation-2 loss, and separates the forecasting decision into branch selection and current-smoothness selection:
\[
\widehat j_T=\psi(V_1,\ldots,V_J),
\qquad
\widehat S_T=\phi(V_{\widehat j_T}).
\]

This decomposition makes several empirically testable strategies explicit. The current smoothness may be the newest local minimum on the selected branch, a recent mean or median, or a loss- and recency-weighted summary. These alternatives can be compared without changing the underlying branch history or the untouched outer test.

Whatever rule is used, the operational estimator is always refit with the newest information available before forecasting. Thus the method uses historical forecast performance to choose regularization while preserving a clean information boundary.

The central empirical question is now whether temporal persistence in local forecast-smoothness minima contains useful information beyond a single pooled optimum. The companion numerical paper addresses the separate problem of recovering and tracking those minima reliably. Together, the two papers separate the forecasting decision from the numerical machinery required to support it.
