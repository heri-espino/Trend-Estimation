\section{Conclusion}

Controlled-smoothness PLS makes the amount of trend regularization interpretable, but leaves open how that smoothness should be chosen when the trend is used for forecasting. This paper proposes a direct answer: choose smoothness by the chronological future-block forecast loss generated by the same penalized trend and continuation rule that will be used operationally.

The resulting object,
\[
S^\star_{d,L,h}
\in
\arg\min_{S\in[0,1]}F_{d,L,h}(S),
\]
is conditional on a clearly specified forecasting configuration. The normalized smoothness scale maps the full penalty path to $[0,1]$, has an exact effective-degrees-of-freedom interpretation, preserves the minimizers of the original penalty parameterization, and includes the two limiting smoothing models explicitly.

The proposal sits at the intersection of three established ideas: controlled smoothness in finite-difference PLS, predictive selection of smoothing and tuning parameters, and rolling out-of-sample forecast evaluation. Its contribution is their combination into a horizon-specific smoothness criterion for penalized trend forecasting.

The empirical question is therefore not whether forecast-based tuning is universally superior to classical smoothing criteria. It is when aligning the smoothness target with the actual forecast horizon changes the selected trend enough to improve later predictions. The simulation and public-data protocol in this paper is designed to answer that question while keeping recovery performance, forecast performance, and numerical optimization conceptually separate.
