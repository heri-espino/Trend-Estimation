\title{Forecast-Optimal Smoothness for Penalized Trend Estimation}

\author[1]{Heriberto Espino Montelongo}
\authormark{ESPINO MONTELONGO}
\titlemark{FORECAST-OPTIMAL SMOOTHNESS}

\address[1]{\orgname{Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla}, \orgaddress{\state{Puebla}, \country{Mexico}}}
\corres{Corresponding author: Heriberto Espino Montelongo. \email{[corresponding email]}}

\abstract[Abstract]{
Finite-difference penalized least squares provides a simple family of trend estimators indexed by a smoothing penalty, but the amount of smoothness is often chosen for estimation rather than for the forecast task that follows. Building on controlled-smoothness formulations of penalized trend estimation, this paper defines a normalized smoothness coordinate $S\in[0,1]$ and selects it directly from chronological future-block forecast performance. For fixed difference order $d$, estimation-window length $L$, and forecast horizon $h$, the proposed criterion averages genuinely out-of-sample squared forecast errors across rolling origins and defines forecast-optimal smoothness as the minimizer of that loss. The construction preserves temporal ordering and uses the continuation implied by the same finite-difference model used to estimate the trend. We establish the monotone correspondence between the penalty parameter and normalized smoothness, derive the exact relation between smoothness and effective degrees of freedom, characterize the two limiting smoothing regimes, and show that optimization in penalty space and smoothness space is equivalent. The contribution is distinct from classical CV/GCV smoothing criteria and from kernel time-series cross-validation: the target is explicitly a horizon-specific future-block forecast loss for finite-difference penalized trends. The accompanying evaluation protocol compares this target with conventional smoothing-parameter rules under controlled trend mechanisms and public forecasting data.
}

\bmsection*{Data availability}
The methodological development does not depend on proprietary data. The submission-stage empirical evaluation is designed around public forecasting data and reproducible real-time or archived series when historical vintages matter. Any exploratory current-vintage macroeconomic results are treated separately from the submission-grade real-time-vintage analysis.

\bmsection*{Code availability}
Reusable implementations of finite-difference penalized trend estimation, chronological forecast objectives, rolling-origin validation, and smoothness transformations are maintained in the \texttt{trend\_estimation} package at \url{https://github.com/heri-espino/Trend-Estimation}. The repository also contains the manuscript source, experiment protocols, and frozen result artifacts used for the paper.
