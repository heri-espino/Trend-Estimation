\title{Forecast-Optimal Smoothness Selection by Chronological Cross-Validation}

\author{Heriberto Espino Montelongo\\
Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla, Puebla, Mexico}
\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A penalized least-squares smoother can fit a historical trend without
selecting the smoothness best suited to extrapolation. We formulate
a forecast-targeted cross-validation method in the normalized
smoothness coordinate $S\in[0,1]$, which maps the unbounded
penalty range $\lambda\in[0,\infty]$ onto a compact scale with
two meaningful limiting trend models. For each candidate $S$,
the smoothing matrix $H(S)$ is applied to a historical training
window and its fitted terminal differences are continued as
a polynomial trend over the intended $h$-step forecast horizon.
The selected smoothness minimizes chronological future-block
mean squared error across historical origins. All validation
outcomes precede the operational forecast, and the chosen
trend is refitted on the most recent observations. The
underlying PLS estimator and trace-based smoothness index
are established constructions: the methodological object
is horizon-matched \emph{predictive} selection on the bounded
smoothness scale, rather than post-hoc trend reconstruction.
In 3,000 controlled simulation scenarios with 72,000
outer-origin--horizon evaluations, the horizon-matched
selector outperforms one-step tuning and GCV in aggregate,
especially at longer horizons. The loss surface need not be
unimodal, so interior candidates and both exact endpoints
must be considered. As a separate optional extension,
historical minima can define time-adaptive smoothness rules,
but broader financial and changing-roughness evaluations
do not establish their general superiority over pooled
forecast cross-validation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} forecast cross-validation; horizon-matched mean squared error; penalized least squares; smoothness index; trend extrapolation.

\section*{Data availability}
The numerical experiments and their fixed simulation seeds are documented in the accompanying repository. The financial evaluation uses a frozen historical market-data snapshot with a machine-readable manifest; data availability and licensing remain subject to the original providers. Results using revised macroeconomic series are exploratory rather than claims of real-time-vintage forecasting performance.

\section*{Code availability}
Reusable implementations of finite-difference penalized trend estimation, chronological forecast objectives, rolling-origin validation, and smoothness transformations are maintained in the \texttt{trend\_estimation} package at \url{https://github.com/heri-espino/Trend-Estimation}. The repository also contains the manuscript source, experiment protocols, and frozen result artifacts used for the paper.
