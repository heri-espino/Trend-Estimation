\title{Forecast-Optimal Smoothness Selection by Chronological Cross-Validation}

\author{Heriberto Espino Montelongo\\
Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla, Puebla, Mexico}
\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Finite-difference penalized least squares produces trends with a continuum
of smoothness levels. Selecting that smoothness for extrapolation is a
different task from recovering a historical signal. We propose a
horizon-matched, chronological forecast cross-validation criterion:
for each normalized smoothness value, smooth the historical training
window, extrapolate the trend over a specified $h$-step validation
block, and minimize the pooled historical forecast mean squared error.
After selection, the final trend is refitted using the latest
observations before forecasting an untouched future block. The
smoothness index and the penalized smoother are established
constructions; the methodological focus is their explicit
forecast-targeted selection and evaluation. A controlled simulation
comprising 3,000 scenarios and 72,000 outer-origin--horizon
evaluations favors horizon-matched selection over one-step forecast
tuning and GCV in aggregate. The objective may have several local minima, requiring an
endpoint-aware search without a unimodality assumption.
We also examine an optional time-adaptive extension that constructs
smoothness decisions from histories of tracked minima. Results
on larger financial panels and changing-roughness simulations do
not establish general superiority of those adaptive rules over the
pooled criterion. Thus forecast-optimal smoothness selection is
a self-contained method, while dynamic branch selection is a
further, assumption-dependent option.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} forecast cross-validation; horizon-matched mean squared error; penalized least squares; smoothness index; trend extrapolation.

\section*{Data availability}
The numerical experiments and their fixed simulation seeds are documented in the accompanying repository. The financial evaluation uses a frozen historical market-data snapshot with a machine-readable manifest; data availability and licensing remain subject to the original providers. Results using revised macroeconomic series are exploratory rather than claims of real-time-vintage forecasting performance.

\section*{Code availability}
Reusable implementations of finite-difference penalized trend estimation, chronological forecast objectives, rolling-origin validation, and smoothness transformations are maintained in the \texttt{trend\_estimation} package at \url{https://github.com/heri-espino/Trend-Estimation}. The repository also contains the manuscript source, experiment protocols, and frozen result artifacts used for the paper.
