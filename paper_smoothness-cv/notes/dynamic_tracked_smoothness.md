# Dynamic tracked smoothness — canonical formulation

**Status: central research direction for `paper_smoothness-cv/`.**

This note supersedes the interpretation that the paper's final method is only
the global minimizer of one pooled forecast-CV surface. That pooled selector
remains an important baseline.

## 1. Local forecast-loss surfaces through time

For a fixed difference order `d`, window length `L`, and forecast horizon `h`,
consider chronological origins `t=1,...,M`. At each origin we construct a
forecast-loss surface over normalized smoothness:

\[
F_t^{(d,L,h)}(S),\qquad S\in[0,1].
\]

Let its local minima be

\[
\mathcal M_t^{(d,L,h)}
=
\{S_{1,t},\ldots,S_{K_t,t}\}.
\]

The scientific object is not only the global minimizer at one time. We want to
study whether local minima form persistent smoothness regimes/branches through
time.

## 2. Data-driven branch tracking

A local minimum at time `t` may continue a branch observed at `t-1` when

\[
|S_{j,t}-S_{j,t-1}|\le \varepsilon.
\]

`epsilon` is a continuation radius in normalized smoothness. It does **not**
predefine a neighborhood such as 'the 0.9 group'. The branch location is
determined by the minima generated by the data; epsilon only controls whether
two consecutive minima are close enough to be treated as the same branch.

Matching must be one-to-one at each origin so two previous branches cannot
collapse onto the same current minimum.

Denote a tracked branch by

\[
\mathcal V_j
=
\{(S_{j,t},\ell^{(1)}_{j,t},\ell^{(2)}_{j,t}):t\in\mathcal T_j\}.
\]

Equivalently, store it as the matrix

\[
V_j=
\begin{pmatrix}
S_{j,t_1} & \ell^{(1)}_{j,t_1} & \ell^{(2)}_{j,t_1}\\
S_{j,t_2} & \ell^{(1)}_{j,t_2} & \ell^{(2)}_{j,t_2}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here:

- `S_{j,t}` is the local-minimum smoothness;
- `ell1` is its Validation-1 forecast loss, the loss that defines the local
  surface at that origin;
- `ell2` is Validation-2 forecast loss after refitting through Validation 1
  with the same `d,L,S` and forecasting the immediately following block.

Additional columns such as date/origin, delta-S, support, curvature, lambda,
and effective degrees of freedom may be stored, but the three columns above
are the minimal scientific state.

## 3. Two distinct decisions

The dynamic method separates:

### A. Which branch should be used?

A branch selector

\[
\widehat j_T=\psi(\mathcal V_1,\ldots,\mathcal V_J)
\]

uses **historical information only**. The current baseline is persistence plus
mean historical Validation-2 loss: prefer complete-support branches; otherwise
compare branches with maximum support, and choose the smallest mean Val2 loss.

Other branch scores may later be studied, but they must be frozen before the
outer test.

### B. Given the selected branch, which smoothness should be used now?

Define

\[
\widehat S_T=\phi(V_{\widehat j_T}).
\]

The central empirical comparison should include at least:

1. **Last**
   \[\phi_{\text{last}}(V_j)=S_{j,T}.\]
2. **Mean-K**
   \[\phi_{\text{mean},K}(V_j)=K^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}S_{j,T-r}.\]
3. **Median-K**
   \[\phi_{\text{med},K}(V_j)=\operatorname{median}\{S_{j,T-K+1:T}\}.\]
4. **Validation-2 weighted**
   \[
   \phi_{\text{V2}}(V_j)
   =
   \frac{\sum_t w_{j,t}S_{j,t}}{\sum_t w_{j,t}},
   \qquad
   w_{j,t}=\frac{1}{\ell^{(2)}_{j,t}+\delta}.
   \]
5. **Recency + Validation-2 weighted**
   \[
   w_{j,t}=\frac{\rho^{T-t}}{\ell^{(2)}_{j,t}+\delta}.
   \]
6. **Predicted smoothness** as a later extension: fit a simple model to the
   branch trajectory and forecast its next `S` rather than using the last one.

`Last` is the simplest dynamic rule and is already implemented by the
`tracked_minima` experiment. Mean/median recent summaries are already stored
as diagnostics but are not yet used as final selectors.

## 4. Mandatory final refit

No rule above averages old trend fits. Once `S_hat_T` has been produced, the
trend is estimated again using the newest information available before the
outer test:

\[
\widehat\tau_T
=
H_{\lambda(\widehat S_T)}y_{T-L+1:T}.
\]

Then

\[
\widehat y_{T+1:T+h\mid T}
=
G_{d,h}\widehat\tau_T.
\]

The untouched future block is used only for scoring.

## 5. Relationship to the pooled forecast-CV baseline

The existing pooled criterion

\[
F_{T,h}^{\mathrm{pool}}(S)
=
\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}\ell_{m,h}(S)
\]

and

\[
\widehat S_{T,h}^{\mathrm{pool}}
\in\arg\min_S F_{T,h}^{\mathrm{pool}}(S)
\]

remain a primary baseline. They answer: *which single smoothness has the best
average historical forecast loss?*

The tracked formulation asks a different question: *do the local minima of the
forecast-loss surface persist through time, and can their branch history be
used to choose a better current smoothness?*

These must not be conflated.

## 6. Ownership across the two active papers

`paper_smoothness-cv/` owns the statistical/forecasting decision problem:
branch definition as a forecasting object, branch selection `psi`, final
smoothness rule `phi`, chronological validation, and out-of-sample forecasting
evidence.

`paper_numerical-methods/` owns numerical recovery of all relevant local
minima on each surface and reliable correspondence/tracking of minima across
nearby surfaces. Brent/adaptive subdivision/Sturm belong there.

Only these two papers are active.
