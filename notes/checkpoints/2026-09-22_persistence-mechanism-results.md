## Scope guardrail

These results explain **one mechanism** inside the active adaptive-forecasting
program. They do not redefine the project around AR(1) persistence.

The canonical object remains

\[
\Theta^\star_{T,h}
=
(d^\star_{T,h},L^\star_{T,h},S^\star_{T,h})
=
G(h,X_T,\mathcal C).
\]

The persistence experiment primarily diagnoses movement in \(S^\star\) under a
controlled DGP. Its oracle AR-aware forecast uses known simulation \(\phi\) and
is not the proposed feasible real-data model. Read
\`notes/research_objective.md\` before using this checkpoint to summarize the
paper.

## Design

- 30 seeds;
- 9 AR(1) coefficients:
  (-0.8,-0.4,0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8,0.9,0.95);
- horizons (hin{1,2,3,6,12});
- 1350 parameter configurations;
- 26,730 outer-origin rows;
- elapsed time about 1281 s (21.3 min);
- fixed DGP values:
  (sigma_{m slope}=0.01),
  (sigma_{arepsilon}=0.5).

At each outer origin, the same selected difference order and window were used to
compare four lambda objectives:

1. observed-series forecasting;
2. oracle latent-trend forecasting;
3. oracle AR-residual-aware forecasting;
4. oracle latent-trend recovery.

## Main decomposition

The experiment supports a decomposition of the first factorial result.

For strong positive persistence, the observed-series forecast optimum is much
less smooth than either latent-trend forecasting or latent-trend recovery.

Example, phi=0.8 and h=1:

[
S^star_{m observed}=0.339,
qquad
S^star_{m AR}=0.593,
qquad
S^star_{m latent}=0.798,
qquad
S^star_{m recovery}=0.940.
]

Thus the ordering is

[
oxed{
S^star_{m observed}
<
S^star_{m AR}
<
S^star_{m latent}
<
S^star_{m recovery}
}
]

for this regime.

The AR-aware objective closes about 55% of the observed-to-latent smoothness
gap at phi=0.8, h=1.

At phi=0.9, h=1:

[
S^star_{m observed}=0.345,
quad
S^star_{m AR}=0.604,
quad
S^star_{m latent}=0.752,
quad
S^star_{m recovery}=0.951.
]

The AR-aware objective closes about 64% of the observed-to-latent gap.

At phi=0.95, h=1 it closes about 79% of that gap.

This supports the interpretation that a trend-only forecast compensates for
omitted persistent residual dynamics by selecting less smoothing. Once a known
AR residual forecast is supplied, the trend can be substantially smoother.

## The mechanism is only partial

The AR correction does not generally move all the way to the latent or recovery
optimum.

This reveals a second separation:

[
S^star_{m latent forecast}

eq
S^star_{m recovery}.
]

Even when the target is the true latent trend, the smoothness that forecasts the
future latent trend need not equal the smoothness that best reconstructs the
historical latent trend.

Therefore the first simulation gap is not generated by one mechanism but at
least two:

1. predictable residual dynamics;
2. the distinction between forecasting a latent trend and reconstructing it.

## Horizon effect

Averaging across phi:

| h | observed S | AR-aware S | latent S | recovery S |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.672 | 0.783 | 0.864 | 0.953 |
| 2 | 0.726 | 0.814 | 0.882 | 0.959 |
| 3 | 0.774 | 0.840 | 0.898 | 0.962 |
| 6 | 0.842 | 0.881 | 0.923 | 0.967 |
| 12 | 0.895 | 0.917 | 0.942 | 0.969 |

The observed, AR-aware, and latent forecasting optima all converge toward high
smoothness as horizon increases. The differences are largest at short horizons.

## Sign of persistence matters

Negative persistence behaves differently.

For phi=-0.8, h=1:

[
S^star_{m observed}=0.993,
quad
S^star_{m AR}=0.970,
quad
S^star_{m latent}=0.971,
quad
S^star_{m recovery}=0.955.
]

The trend-only procedure oversmooths rather than undersmooths. The AR-aware
smoothness is almost exactly the latent-forecast smoothness.

This is useful evidence against a variance-only explanation. The sign and
dynamics of serial dependence matter.

## Forecast accuracy of the oracle AR diagnostic

The oracle AR-residual-aware forecast improves observed-series RMSE in the
strong-persistence cases. For example:

- phi=-0.8, h=1: RMSE ratio AR/trend-only = 0.672;
- phi=0.8, h=1: 0.960;
- phi=0.9, h=6: 0.925;
- phi=0.95, h=12: 0.913.

These are mechanism diagnostics because phi is known from the simulation; they
are not yet feasible real-data performance claims.

## Important numerical caveat: boundary selections

A substantial fraction of optima occur at the current search boundaries
(loglambdain[-10,16]).

Across all 26,730 outer origins:

- observed objective:
  2392 lower-bound and 3341 upper-bound selections;
- latent objective:
  632 lower-bound and 1511 upper-bound selections;
- AR-aware objective:
  2145 lower-bound and 3842 upper-bound selections;
- recovery objective:
  338 upper-bound selections.

The concentration is highly regime-dependent.

Examples:

- phi=-0.8, h=1: observed optimum is at the upper boundary in 56.8% of rows;
- phi=-0.4, h=1: upper boundary in 46.5%;
- phi=0.8, h=1: lower boundary in 26.2%;
- phi=0.9, h=3: lower boundary in 29.8%;
- phi=0.95, h=1: lower boundary in 27.3%.

Therefore the qualitative persistence pattern is strong, but the exact
smoothness levels must not yet be treated as final. The next required experiment
is a search-domain sensitivity run with materially wider log-lambda bounds.

## Current interpretation

The strongest provisional result is:

[
oxed{
	ext{forecast-optimal smoothness depends on what part of the observed process
must be carried by the trend forecast.}
}
]

When predictable residual dynamics are omitted, the trend smoother can absorb
them by changing its smoothness. Forecast-optimal smoothing is therefore a
property of the forecasting architecture, horizon, and stochastic dependence,
not simply a property of the latent trend itself.
