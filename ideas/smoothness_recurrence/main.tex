\begin{abstract}
This applied study compares several established ways to define and forecast a
financial trend under a common chronological validation and untouched-test
protocol. Candidate methods include forecast-optimal penalized trends,
classical smoothness selection, likelihood/state-space trends,
autoregressive/ARIMA benchmarks, residual autoregression, and a no-change
baseline. For every method, the trend forecast path is frozen at the forecast
origin. We then study first-passage and recurrence behavior of future prices
relative to those ex-ante paths. The central empirical question is whether
forecast performance and measured recurrence are robust to the definition of
trend. The numerical smoothness-selection algorithm used by one candidate is
developed separately and is not a contribution of this paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Competing definitions of forecast trend}

At forecast origin $T$, method $m$ uses only information available through
$T$ to construct
\[
\widehat\tau^{(m)}_{T+k\mid T},
\qquad k\ge0.
\]
Candidate method families are intentionally limited to distinct modeling
principles rather than a large forecasting model zoo.

\section{Chronological validation and test}

Development/validation data are used for all model-specific choices. Once the
protocol and model configuration are frozen, forecasts are evaluated on an
untouched test block. Test outcomes do not feed back into model selection.

\section{Recurrence relative to a frozen trend path}

For log price $x_t$, define
\[
g^{(m)}_{T,k}
=
x_{T+k}
-
\widehat\tau^{(m)}_{T+k\mid T}.
\]
A crossing time is
\[
H_T^{(m)}
=
\inf\left\{
k\ge1:
g^{(m)}_{T,k}g^{(m)}_{T,0}\le0
\right\}.
\]
Additional outcomes may include tolerance-band entry, probability of
recurrence by horizon, restricted-mean recurrence time, and survival/hazard
summaries under right censoring.

\section{Research questions}

The study asks which trend definitions forecast well on untouched data,
whether the preferred method changes with horizon, whether recurrence
statistics are robust to the trend definition, and how recurrence depends on
initial standardized distance from trend.

\section{Scope}

The paper is empirical/applied. It does not develop the numerical
stationary-point search for normalized smoothness and does not study adaptive
time-varying joint selection of $(d,L,S)$. Recurrence to a forecast trend is not
automatically interpreted as stationary mean reversion or trading
profitability.
