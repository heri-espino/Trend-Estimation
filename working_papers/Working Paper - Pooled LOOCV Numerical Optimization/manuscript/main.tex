\begin{abstract}
Penalized least-squares trend smoothing requires a choice of its
regularization parameter. Following the experimental setting of
Cort\'es-Toto, Guerrero and Reyes (2017), we investigate the numerical
optimization of leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) loss for one
or more comparable observed time series. We define a pooled criterion,
derive its finite-parameter derivative and exact endpoint limits,
and specify comparisons between bounded scalar minimization,
multi-bracket Brent root refinement and restricted exact Sturm
root isolation. A completed source-inspired pilot is complemented by a separate
stochastic-trend simulation comparing observed-series LOOCV against
the known noise-variance ratio in Guerrero's model. A financial
illustration remains a prospective application. No accuracy or
efficiency superiority result is asserted at this stage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and literature positioning}
Trend smoothing by penalized least squares (PLS) is classical
\citep{guerrero2007,cortes2017}. Classical LOOCV and GCV
are already used for choosing smoothing parameters
\citep{cortes2017,weinert2007}.
A new numerical contribution requires a demonstrated improvement
over existing global GCV optimization, including the prior-art
investigation discussed in the research notebook.
We address observed-series reconstruction, not prediction.

\section{Pooled LOOCV and normalized smoothness}
Let $y^{(r)}\in\mathbb R^N$ for $r=1,\dots,R$, with
$N>d\geq1$, $D_d$ the forward difference matrix,
$Q=D_d^\top D_d$, and
\begin{equation}
 H_\lambda=(I_N+\lambda Q)^{-1}, \qquad
 \widehat\tau_\lambda^{(r)}=H_\lambda y^{(r)}.
\end{equation}
For $\lambda>0$, define the pooled LOOCV MSE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pooled}
 F_R(\lambda)=\frac1{RN}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
 \left(\frac{y_i^{(r)}-(H_\lambda y^{(r)})_i}
 {1-(H_\lambda)_{ii}}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
This averages errors \emph{before} minimizing;
it is not the average of $R$ optimum regularization parameters.
The normalized Guerrero-type coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 S(\lambda)=\frac{N-\operatorname{tr}H_\lambda}{N-d}
 \in[0,1].
\end{equation}
For $R=1$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pooled} is ordinary LOOCV.

\section{Endpoint limits and analytical derivatives}
Put $T_\lambda=Q(I+\lambda Q)^{-1}$. Since
$I-H_\lambda=\lambda T_\lambda$, LOOCV residuals satisfy
\begin{equation}
 e_{ri}(\lambda)=\frac{(T_\lambda y^{(r)})_i}
 {(T_\lambda)_{ii}} .
\end{equation}
The continuous extension at $\lambda=0$ is
$e_{ri}(0)=(Qy^{(r)})_i/Q_{ii}$. At
$\lambda=\infty$, write $P$ for the orthogonal projector onto
$\ker Q$ and use
$e_{ri}(\infty)=[(I-P)y^{(r)}]_i/(1-P_{ii})$.
For finite $\lambda$, $T'_\lambda=-T_\lambda^2$ and hence
\begin{equation}
 F_R'(\lambda)=\frac{2}{RN}\sum_{r,i}e_{ri}(\lambda)
 \frac{-(T_\lambda^2y^{(r)})_i(T_\lambda)_{ii}
 +(T_\lambda y^{(r)})_i(T_\lambda^2)_{ii}}
 {(T_\lambda)_{ii}^2}.
\end{equation}
These are standard linear-smoother calculus identities; proofs
and numerical edge cases are recorded in notes/PROOF_LEDGER.md.

\section{Numerical candidate recovery}
We plan to compare (i) a finite dense log-$\lambda$ grid,
(ii) one bounded Brent-style scalar search,
(iii) multiple sign-change brackets of the derivative followed
by Brent root refinement and evaluation of both endpoints,
and (iv) exact Sturm isolation for small rational instances.
Only method (iv), subject to exact-arithmetic restrictions and
correct root classification, supplies exact positive-root counts.
A derivative-sign scan alone cannot guarantee completeness.

\section{Simulation design and financial illustration}
Use the four-factor design of \citet{cortes2017}:
linear or nonlinear Beta-mixture trend, seasonality present/absent,
Gaussian noise standard deviation 0.5/2, and $N=50/200$.
Pools with $R>1$ are an independently declared extension
using a common latent trend and independent noisy replicates.
Predeclare paired comparisons of candidate recovery, objective
values, runtime, boundary selection and sensitivity to pooling.
A financial case study may illustrate retrospective smoothing,
without forecasting or investment-performance claims.

\subsection{Known noise-variance ratio under Guerrero's model}

To distinguish numerical LOOCV optimization from model-informed trend
estimation, we introduce a \emph{separate} stochastic design following
\citet{guerrero2007}. For mutually uncorrelated disturbances, let
\begin{equation}
 y^{(r)}=\tau^{(r)}+\eta^{(r)},\quad
 D_d\tau^{(r)}=\varepsilon^{(r)},\quad
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta^{(r)})=\sigma_\eta^2I,\quad
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon^{(r)})=\sigma_\varepsilon^2I.
\end{equation}
The population variance ratio motivates the model-informed parameter
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_G=\frac{\sigma_\eta^2}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}.
\end{equation}
The benchmark is given \emph{true simulation variances}, not estimated
variance components from empirical financial observations. We compare
$\lambda_G$ with the pooled LOOCV selections produced by one bounded
Brent run, multibracket derivative Brent, and a finite dense grid.

For each choice $\lambda_m$ we measure (i) observed-series
$F_R(\lambda_m)$ and (ii) the latent-trend reconstruction criterion
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{TrendMSE}(\lambda_m)=
 \frac{1}{RN}\sum_{r=1}^R
 \|H_{\lambda_m}y^{(r)}-\tau^{(r)}\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The simulated latent trend is \emph{never} used to select $\lambda$
under LOOCV. The optimization objective and the reconstruction target
are different; obtaining the smallest observed-sample CV criterion
does not establish the smallest realized or expected latent-trend
error. The fixed polynomial initialization of the integrated trend
should not be conflated with a proper finite-variance prior for
its unpenalized nullspace coefficients.

\subsection{Exploratory known-ratio pilot (not confirmatory)}
A separate stochastic-trend pilot completed $12$ settings,
each with five randomly seeded repetitions and pool sizes $R=1$
and $R=3$, yielding $120$ nested pool evaluations and four method
rows per evaluation. The two pool sizes share the first generated
series at matching seeds; hence they cannot be treated as
independent samples in a pooled inferential analysis.

For the $R=3$ group (60 pool evaluations), the mean observed
LOOCV scores were $2.697581$ for the known Guerrero variance
ratio and $2.681409$ for the multi-bracket LOOCV selection.
The mean \emph{true trend} MSEs were instead $0.385673$
and $0.394848$, respectively. In $45$ of those $60$
paired pools the Guerrero ratio attained lower realized
trend MSE. This is a known-parameter comparison;
the advantage cannot be attributed to an estimated variance
component method.

In this stochastic pilot, both bounded Brent and
multi-bracket Brent attained effectively identical LOOCV
objectives in every pool at a tolerance of $10^{-7}$.
All $60$ $R=3$ objectives yielded only one
numerically detected derivative-sign root under the
finite scan. This count is \emph{not} a global certificate
or a proof of unimodality. Therefore, this particular
DGP provides evidence for the distinction between
statistical and numerical targets, but no measured
numerical superiority of multi-bracket search over the
single-start procedure. The results require a genuinely
independent confirmatory experiment and objective-specific
numerical verification before journal-level claims.

\subsection{Feasible estimation of both variance components: exploratory pilot}

The preceding known-ratio comparison assumes the true variance
components are supplied. This is not a feasible smoothing selector
for ordinary data. Under the additional independent Gaussian
stochastic-difference model and $V=I$, put $z^{(r)}=D_2y^{(r)}$ and
$A=D_2D_2^\top$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(z^{(r)})=
 \sigma_\varepsilon^2 I+\sigma_\eta^2 A.
\]
We independently implement two \emph{feasible extensions}: (a)
Gaussian maximum likelihood of the differenced observations, with
the common covariance scale profiled analytically, and (b) two
quadratic moment equations with nonnegative least-squares
constraints. These estimators are \emph{not} attributed to
Guerrero's published smoothness-selection procedure.

A distinct exploratory pilot considered $N\in\{50,200\}$,
$\sigma_\eta\in\{0.5,2\}$,
$\sigma_\varepsilon\in\{0.1,0.3,1\}$,
five independently seeded pools per cell, and nested $R=1$ and $R=3$.
For the primary $R=3$ subset (60 pools), the mean
observed-series LOOCV and latent-trend MSE results were:

Gaussian likelihood achieved lower realized trend MSE than bounded
LOOCV in 37 of 60 $R=3$ paired pools. It estimated observation-noise
and trend-innovation variance with average relative absolute errors
of approximately 10.0\% and 18.3\%, respectively. The error rates
fell with increasing sample length in this particular simulation.

The constrained moment method set its estimated trend-innovation
variance to zero in 16 of 60 $R=3$ pools, implying
$\widehat\lambda=\infty$ and fitting a linear polynomial even
when the stochastic trend had nonzero second differences. Such
catastrophic errors make its mean latent-trend MSE unreliable as a
measure of typical numerical approximation accuracy; we report
failure rates and medians as well. The comparison is necessarily
exploratory with just five seeds per DGP cell, and results
do not establish an expected-risk inequality for all noise
configurations. The $R=1$ observations are nested in $R=3$,
not independent replications. Complete per-cell results and
replication details are retained in the corresponding notes.

$\phi=0.65$, and observations with unequal half-sample variances

Each regime covered $N\in\{50,200\}$, $\sigma_\eta\in\{0.5,2\}$,
$\sigma_\varepsilon=0.3$, five independent replicate seeds and

In the AR(1) group, single-observation LOOCV attained a
\emph{lower observed} CV score ($0.913917$ rather than $1.035299$)
but much worse latent trend reconstruction than Gaussian difference
likelihood. Serially dependent observation errors violate the
independent-deletion interpretation of ordinary LOOCV. The
difference-Gaussian likelihood also has misspecified covariance,
and its observed relative performance should not be attributed
to a covariance-correct optimality theorem. Moreover, the nominal
$\sigma_\eta^2/\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ reference is not an oracle for
correlated or time-varying measurement covariance.

For single-series $R=1$ with Student-$t_5$ trend innovations,
Gaussian likelihood instead had a larger mean true-trend MSE
than LOOCV ($0.392287$ versus $0.324086$), despite lower error
in 11 of 20 paired realizations. Hence neither feasible selector
uniformly dominates under misspecification.

equal to a numerical tolerance of $10^{-7}$ in all 100 pooled
$R=3$ evaluations; no numerical advantage of multibracket
search emerged. The nonnegative-moments estimator selected
$\widehat\sigma_\varepsilon^2=0$ in 30 of 100 $R=3$ pools,
demonstrating frequent boundary oversmoothing in this
specific simple variance estimator.

All these summaries are based on only five independent seeds
per DGP cell, with $R=1$ nested within $R=3$. Neither
statistical significance nor generalized solver superiority
is inferred. A dependence-aware or covariance-aware benchmark
is needed before further conclusions about the AR(1) case.

\subsection{Matched-order and skew-normal tail-noise pilot}

Before running the follow-up tail-shape experiment, we extended its
design to three matched generating and fitting orders
$d\in\{1,2,3\}$. For each $d$, stochastic trends are generated
with $D_d\tau=\varepsilon$ and then smoothed with
$H_{\lambda,d}=(I+\lambda D_d^\top D_d)^{-1}$. At the infinite
penalty limit the fitted trend belongs to the polynomial
nullspace of degree at most $d-1$. This controlled design changes
the generating model with $d$, so it is \emph{not} an
unknown-order selection experiment.

The new noise designs include centered Gamma distributions of
either skew sign, Student-$t_3$ shocks, contaminated Gaussian
mixtures, Student-$t_5$ GARCH-type volatility and Cauchy shocks.
We additionally test centered, variance-standardized Azzalini
skew-normal shocks with shape $\alpha=-6,-3,0,3,6$.
Writing $\delta=\alpha/\sqrt{1+\alpha^2}$ and
$Z=\delta|U|+\sqrt{1-\delta^2}V$ for independent
$U,V\sim N(0,1)$, the observation disturbance is
\[
 \eta=\sigma_\eta
 \frac{Z-\delta\sqrt{2/\pi}}{\sqrt{1-2\delta^2/\pi}}.
\]
Thus skewness varies with $\alpha$ while population mean and
variance remain $0$ and $\sigma_\eta^2$, respectively.

The executed exploratory pilot used the predeclared factorial:
13 distribution settings, three values of $d$, two series lengths
and two noise scales, five seeds per cell and nested pool sizes
$R=1,3$ (1,560 paired pool evaluations and 4,680 method rows).
Results are stratified by $d$, since integrated trend
magnitudes vary greatly by order. For Cauchy observation noise,
variance and population squared-error risk are undefined;
there is no true Guerrero variance ratio and only robust
finite-realization error summaries are appropriate.
This experiment is distinct from the completed five-regime
robustness pilot reported above.

\subsection{Sensitivity to nonzero mean trend differences}

The preceding stochastic-trend experiments set the population
mean of the penalized trend differences equal to zero.
This is a modeling restriction, not a requirement of
Guerrero's framework: Guerrero (2007) already allows nonzero
mean differences and proposes an observed-difference sample
mean plug-in. We therefore prespecify a separate
\emph{observed-series reconstruction} experiment to measure
the consequences of a misspecified zero-difference mean.

For a fixed analyst-chosen order $d=2$, assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonzero-mu-dgp}
 y^{(r)}=\tau^{(r)}+\eta^{(r)},\qquad
 D_d\tau^{(r)}=\mu\mathbf1+\varepsilon^{(r)},\qquad
 \varepsilon^{(r)}\sim N(0,aI),\quad
 \eta^{(r)}\sim N(0,bI),
\end{equation}
with independent centered innovations and independent
series $r=1,\ldots,R$. Let $p_j=\binom jd$ for zero-indexed $j$
so that $D_dp=\mathbf1$. For an externally fixed mean,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonzero-mu-affine}
 \widehat\tau_{\lambda,\mu}
 =\arg\min_{\tau}
 \{\|y-\tau\|^2+\lambda\|D_d\tau-\mu\mathbf1\|^2\}
 =H_\lambda y+\mu(I-H_\lambda)p.
\end{equation}
This well-known affine form is not claimed as a novelty.
For $d=2$ the $\lambda\to\infty$ fit has constant
second differences $\mu$, rather than a linear nullspace
fit if $\mu=0$ is imposed.

Let $z_r=D_dy_r$, $A=D_dD_d^\top$, and
$\Sigma=aI+bA$. Under the Gaussian differenced-data model,
$z_r\sim N(\mu\mathbf1,\Sigma)$. Conditional on a fixed
covariance, the common generalized least-squares mean is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gls-mean-contrast}
 \widehat\mu(\Sigma)=
 \frac{\sum_r\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}z_r}
 {R\,\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}.
\end{equation}
We fit $(\mu,a,b)$ by a profiled Gaussian
\emph{differenced-observation} contrast likelihood;
this is a computational extension, not a new estimator
attributed to Guerrero.

For a separate pooled observed-LOOCV comparison,
we estimate a single common $\mu$ jointly with all $R$
penalized trends \emph{within each training deletion}.
With $T_\lambda=Q(I+\lambda Q)^{-1}$,
$b_\lambda=T_\lambda p$,
$k_\lambda=p^\top T_\lambda p>0$ and
$\overline y=R^{-1}\sum_r y_r$, the joint full-sample
mean is
\[
 \widehat\mu_\lambda=
 \frac{b_\lambda^\top\overline y}{k_\lambda}.
\]
The associated exact pooled leave-one-observation-out
residual, which accounts for re-estimation of $\mu$
after omitting its target, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonzero-mu-fold-clean-press}
 e_{ri}(\lambda)=
 \frac{(T_\lambda y_r)_i-
       b_{\lambda,i}\widehat\mu_\lambda}
 {(T_\lambda)_{ii}-
       b_{\lambda,i}^2/(R k_\lambda)}.
\end{equation}
This differs from computing an all-data $\widehat\mu$ once
and incorrectly treating it as fixed during validation.
The effective degrees of freedom also change upon
estimating $\mu$; the original normalized smoothness
index remains a monotone penalty coordinate but is
not the full data-selected-mean smoother's EDF.

The completed simulation fixes
$\sigma_\varepsilon=0.3$, considers
$\mu/\sigma_\varepsilon\in\{-1,-0.25,0,0.25,1\}$,
$N\in\{50,200\}$, $\sigma_\eta\in\{0.5,2\}$,
and five common-random-number replicate seeds per
$(N,\sigma_\eta)$ with nested $R\in\{1,3\}$.
Each of the five $\mu$ levels reuses the SAME
realized Gaussian trend innovations and observation noises.
The study contains 200 pooled evaluations and 1,600
method rows, including 100 $R=3$ treatment pools but
only \emph{20 distinct underlying R=3 shock draws}.
All eight methods have complete finite realized latent
reconstruction-MSE records.

\subsection{Independent-seed Gaussian confirmation across four orders}

A completed, independently seeded Gaussian
Monte Carlo confirmation extends the Gaussian mean-difference
experiment to four separately analyst-selected orders
$d\in\{1,2,3,4\}$. For each $d$ the correctly matched
generating and fitted trend model is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multorder-dgp}
 D_d\tau^{(r)}=\mu\mathbf1+\varepsilon^{(r)},\quad
 y^{(r)}=\tau^{(r)}+\eta^{(r)},\quad
 \varepsilon^{(r)}\sim N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2I),\quad
 \eta^{(r)}\sim N(0,\sigma_\eta^2I).
\end{equation}
With zero-indexed time and initial trend
$\tau_t=0.02t$ for $t<d$, the realized latent
trajectory, not merely its expectation, has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multorder-latent-trajectory}
 \tau_t=0.02t+\mu\binom{t}{d}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{t-d}\binom{t-j-1}{d-1}\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad t\ge d .
\end{equation}

We keep the eight original selectors and true
retrospective trend-MSE target fixed, while varying
$N\in\{50,200\}$, $\sigma_\eta\in\{0.5,2\}$,
$\sigma_\varepsilon=0.3$, and standardized
$\mu/\sigma_\varepsilon\in\{-1,-0.25,0,0.25,1\}$.
For each of the 16 $(d,N,\sigma_\eta)$ settings,
10,000 distinct, independently seeded three-series
shock draws were generated. The five mean interventions
reuse the \emph{same} original shocks and the
$R=1$ pool reuses the first member of the $R=3$
pool. Thus 160,000 independent three-series shock
draws give 1.6 million nested treatment-pool
evaluations, or 12.8 million per-method records;
these records are \emph{not} independent statistical
replicates.

The completed local campaign stored immutable,
SHA256-identified compressed shards of 100 independent
draws each and checked paired reconstruction-loss
summaries within every $(d,N,\sigma_\eta,\mu,R)$ cell.
Reconstruction performance was compared through
within-seed paired MSE differences, with Monte Carlo
standard errors calculated at the independent
shock-draw level. Six methods are
equivariant to adding the deterministic term
$\mu\binom{t}{d}$ and their fitted results can
be reused algebraically under mean shifts;
the two incorrectly fixed-zero-mean selectors
must be reoptimized at each shift. This is
computational simplification, not additional
replication.

The polynomial drift grows rapidly with
$d$ and $N$, particularly for $d=4$, so
cross-order raw MSE is \emph{not} an estimand
for choosing the best difference order.
A direct SVD of $D_d$ avoids squaring its
condition number when representing small
positive spectral modes at order four.
Results from the originally executed $d=2$
five-seed pilot remain separate and are not
interchangeable with this completed
independent-seed confirmation.

When the true difference mean was zero,
retaining the correct zero-mean restriction
gave slightly lower mean MSE than
estimating one unnecessary mean parameter
in all 16 $R=3$ cells. With only one series

These cases are important
bias--variance counterexamples, not
violations of the structural model.

The five $\mu$ treatments per seed are
dependent affine shifts of the \emph{same}
stochastic realization. The fitted-mean
methods' invariant error as $\mu$ changes
is consequently an algebraic
equivariance property, \emph{not}
five independent repetitions.
This experiment supports the
importance of modeling the mean of
trend differences under this Gaussian
latent-reconstruction DGP, but does
not show distribution-free method
dominance, a unique best difference
order, superior out-of-sample forecasts
or numerical global-optimality guarantees.
A separately prespecified non-Gaussian
mean-shift campaign remains unrun.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\textbf{Gaussian confirmation completed, external robustness pending.}
The independent 10,000-seed-per-cell Gaussian mean-difference
simulation confirms a substantial reconstruction penalty for
incorrectly fixing nonzero mean differences at zero in its
three-series pools. It does not establish a uniformly best
smoothing selector, Gaussian superiority under nonnormality,
a method for selecting the difference order, or a novel
global-optimality theorem. Evidence for dependent/non-Gaussian
errors, application-calibrated signal scales, the relationship
to forecast-horizon selection and generalization to financial
observations remains for separate experiments.
