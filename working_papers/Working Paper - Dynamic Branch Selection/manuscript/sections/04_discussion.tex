\section{Discussion and research status}
\label{sec:branch_discussion}
The empirical results do not establish unconditional superiority
of tracked-minimum decision rules over pooled forecast-CV.
The small reserved confirmation experiment favored a
recency-weighted decision, but the 64-series external
panel and controlled roughness experiments did not
replicate an aggregate improvement. Branch identity
under crossings, births, disappearances, or unequal
origin spacing is also a modeling decision rather
than a unique property of the observed data.

The historical results are frozen under their original
protocols. The present independent working manuscript
documents their limitations, rather than presenting
unrun matching alternatives, newly tuned policies, or
revised simulations as completed. Further work must
evaluate origin spacing, branch-matching accuracy,
forecast loss and smoothness-rule stability under
a fully chronological outer-test design.

\section{Conclusion}
Temporal branch selection is a distinct hypothesis about
the persistence and predictability of desirable smoothing
regimes. It is algebraically feasible to summarize historical
local minima by a branch matrix and choose a future
smoothness via maps $\psi$ and $\phi$, but existence of that
representation does not guarantee forecast improvement.
The recorded negative results remain substantive evidence
about limits of the approach.
